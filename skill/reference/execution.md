# Session Execution

How the athlete runs each session, and how the daily review judges it afterwards (`reviews.md`, "Review yesterday"). The readiness rules that change today's session are in `load-management.md`.

## Easy Sessions

- **The ceiling is the rule.** Easy is Zones 1–2. On runs, the top of Zone 2 is a hard ceiling. On hills, walk when HR passes it. A day in high Zone 1 in heat is fine. Drift into Zone X is the failure to catch (80/20; Seiler).
- **Intensity discipline:** don't chase riders or runners who pass, on the road or in a virtual world. Indoors, ride in ERG mode or hold the power cap (Seiler; coach's experience).
- **One technique focus** per easy session: cadence, relaxed shoulders, run form. Quality isn't intensity: every session is done with attention (Bu; coach's experience).
- **Judge it by the next key session.** If an easy session went harder than planned, say what it may cost the next quality session one or two days later (Bu; coach's experience).
- **Talk test:** full sentences means aerobic. Nose breathing isn't a valid easy-zone governor, because trained people can nose-breathe at fairly high intensities (Seiler; research).

## Quality Sessions

- **Start low and build.** Begin the first rep at the low end of the target zone, about 10 s/km under threshold pace on a run, and build only if it feels sustainable. A tested threshold is a band that moves with the day (`zones.md`, "Reading a test"). Finish with one more rep in the tank (Seiler, Eriksson; coach's experience).
- **HR is a ceiling, not a target,** on run threshold reps. Hold the pace or belt speed steady and don't speed up to chase HR. Judge reps by end-of-rep HR, since HR lags at the start of each rep (Claude's judgement).
- **Recoveries are jogged, not walked,** in run intervals (That Triathlon Show hosts; coach's experience).
- **If the reps feel near maximal,** use the lower half of the target range; if easy, the upper half. When an athlete's zones are out of date, apply this until the next test (Claude's judgement).
- **No races in training.** Save personal bests for key sessions and race day, and keep unplanned tests out (Seiler, Bu; coach's experience).
- **Uncertain day:** start easy and see. Do the warm-up as planned. If the first rep lands on target, continue; if not, switch to easy. One session makes no difference; rushing one can cost the next two weeks (Bu; coach's experience).

## Long Sessions and Heat

- **There is no true steady state.** At constant pace or power, HR and effort climb with time (cardiac drift), more in heat. Let HR govern and let pace drop (Seiler; research).
- **Heat limits power** whatever the fitness: sustainable race power depends on heat, cooling and fuelling, not only FTP. Use HR as the heat governor outdoors, and rehearse cooling and fuelling in key sessions (Bu; coach's experience).
- **Breaks in long easy runs:** a short stop, about 1 minute every 25–30 minutes, lowers lactate at the same average pace and gives a chance to drink (Bakken; coach's experience). It's the athlete's choice and doesn't change the session time.
- **Fuelling and drinking** follow `fuelling.md`.

## Outdoor Rides Without Power

Ride the outdoor HR caps from `zones.md` ("Without a Power Meter Outdoors") plus RPE. Pace outdoor long rides like the race only once HR-at-power has been calibrated in race-pace rides indoors.

## Bike Position and Fit

- **Aero costs some power at first.** Riding on aerobars costs about 0–5% of threshold power in adapted riders and more in newcomers, with a large individual spread. Aerobars cut drag by about 16–21%. Triathletes who train in aero show little or no extra oxygen cost (Peveler 2005; research, cross-sectional).
- **Build aero time in easy rides first.** Use progressively longer Zone 1–2 aero blocks, then threshold reps in aero. Do VO2 and hill reps upright. Coaches split on threshold in aero; no 80/20 guidance was found (Claude's judgement).
- **A road bike with clip-on bars** is the classic closed-hip setup. Fitters often move the saddle forward for it (not checked on any one bike).
- **Changing the fit:** record every measurement before changing anything. Change one thing at a time in small steps, about 5 mm for saddle height. Test each change on a short easy spin before a long ride (Claude's judgement).
- **See a fitter** for front-of-knee pain, a hip pinch, numbness, back pain the day after aero rides, or quad burn in aero that hasn't faded after 3–4 weeks.
- **Cheap speed** sits in position, handling and technique as well as FTP: speed over the distance wins, and power counts only as far as it turns into speed (Bu; coach's experience).

## Run Shoes

- **New shoes are broken in gradually:** worn at home first, then for part of an easy run, adding about 10 minutes per easy run. Quality sessions follow only once easy runs are symptom-free, and long runs come last. First-step morning heel or arch pain means back to the usual shoe (Claude's judgement).
- **Run tests in the usual shoe,** so results compare.
- Bakken saw fewer running injuries in his practice after modern foam and carbon-plate shoes arrived. For an injury-prone runner, a modern shoe or a different brand is worth trying (coach's experience, clinical impression).

## Treadmill Runs

At a given pace a treadmill run costs slightly less energy. Most runners find it harder and run slower than outdoors: one 18-runner study found about 3% less distance in a 1-hour time trial, at lower HR (Fitzgerald, "Don't Trust Your Treadmill"; research, small study). Individuals differ in both directions.

- **Believe the athlete's report** of how the treadmill feels, in either direction, and steer by HR and feel first, pace second.
- **Belt displays are often several percent off,** and drift with wear. A watch's treadmill pace is an estimate. Neither is a cross-check for the other.
- **Never compare treadmill pace with outdoor pace** or with an outdoor run test. Compare treadmill runs only with treadmill runs, ideally on the same machine.
- A 1% incline approximates outdoor energy cost at faster paces (Fitzgerald).
- Keep some outdoor running for heat adaptation before a hot race.

## Reading the Recorded Data

- **Like for like only.** The warm-up HR check and the "lower HR but harder" rule compare a session with the athlete's normal on the same machine, setting and load (`load-management.md`). Treadmill against outdoor HR is a different comparison.
- **Swim times:** the pace clock, push-off to touch, is the source of truth. A watch custom workout that auto-advances steps gives usable rep laps, but each work lap includes some wall time. Third-party re-uploads that count only time inside detected lengths understate pace; don't use them.
- **Duplicate uploads** of one session double-count weekly hours. Count it once, keeping the watch's original.
- **HR dropouts:** a stretch of implausibly low HR while running is a strap contact problem (wet the electrodes, strap snug, battery, worn strap), not physiology. Exclude it from time in zone.
- **Recording intervals:** record the whole session without pausing in recoveries; mark reps with manual laps, or use a custom workout so the watch advances the steps.
- **Watch VO2max estimates** move with the algorithm as much as with fitness; don't read much into them either way (Bu; coach's experience).
- **Swim HR** from a wrist watch in the water is unreliable; judge swim effort by pace-clock times and feel (Edgley; coach's experience).
- **Lactate** is a concentration, so hydration, heat and plasma volume move it. Compare readings only across tests in similar conditions, and never decide on lactate alone (Bu; coach's experience).
- **Few numbers.** On easy days use the HR cap and feel. On quality days use power (bike) or end-of-rep HR (run), plus RPE. Add a metric only if it changes a decision (Seiler's "heads-up display"; coach's experience). Bu prefers raw time, distance and work (kJ) to modelled scores. Bike kJ is a fair guide for sizing long-ride fuel, but kJ ≈ kcal is only approximate.

## Breathing as a Signal

Breathing rate may track effort in intervals better than HR, which lags short surges (Seiler; early research). Stride-breath rhythm shifts from about 4-4 or 3-3 on easy runs toward 2-2 near threshold. If easy running goes to 2-2, the athlete is probably above the cap (not validated). Use it as an extra cue; it doesn't replace HR and RPE.
