# Swim Technique

Swim efficiency is low in everyone, so technique is where cheap speed sits (Bu; coach's experience). Source unless noted: Brenton Ford on That Triathlon Show (coach's experience, no studies cited).

## Fix in Order

Work up from the bottom. Each layer makes the next easier, so the first broken layer is the focus:

1. **Breathing:** exhale before inhaling. Gentle exhale once the face is back in, the biggest exhale as the head turns. Holding the air and topping up makes the swimmer breathless after 20–30 s. A swimmer who "can't finish" a hard 400 checks breathing first.
2. **Body frame:** head looking down to about 45° forward, long and tall, chest open, core lightly braced, so lung buoyancy lifts hips and legs. Low legs are the most common fault.
3. **Kick:** small, narrow, from the hip; for balance and something to press against, not propulsion. A big knee-driven "bicycle" kick drags. Too little kick also lets the legs sink.
4. **Rotation:** about 30–40°, hips, torso and shoulders together. Over-rotation, usually on the breathing stroke, pushes the leading arm down with no catch.
5. **Recovery, entry and reach.**
6. **Catch:** elbow out to the side, fingertips down, forearm facing back (early vertical forearm). The catch sets up the pull; the common fault is a straight, narrow arm and a dropped elbow.
7. **Pull:** slow to fast, about 30–40% effort at the catch rising to 60–70% at the back. From under the shoulder backwards, draw the shoulder blade down and back to use the lats.
8. **Timing:** front-quadrant timing for long swims: one arm in front of the head most of the time.

"Looks good but slow" means the arm, shoulder and rotation aren't connected, or "relaxed" was taken too literally: a relaxed body, not a relaxed pull. Technique work doesn't replace the Zone 3 reps.

## Drills by Fault

| Fault                  | Drill                                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Low hips and legs      | Ball float → streamline float → push off and swim 25 as relaxed as possible              |
| Knee-driven kick       | Kick on the side; kick without a board                                                   |
| Over-rotation          | One-arm freestyle with fins, other arm at the side, breathing to the non-stroking side   |
| Dropped elbow at catch | Short dog paddle ("doggy scoop"); YMCA drill (kick about 5 s in each of 4 arm positions) |
| Shoulders rounded      | Shoulder blade down and back from mid-pull, as a cue on easy laps                        |

## Using Drills

- 200–400 m of drill and swim inside the Zone 1 warm-up of each session; the library distances stay as written.
- Pick 1–2 focuses and keep them 6–8 weeks; the athlete knows why each drill is there.
- Film before and after a block: front-on and side views, with a camera on a suction mount at the pool end. Almost everyone looks different from how they imagine, and drill transfer is individual.
- A few short sprints at the start of a long swim groove technique, not speed (Edgley citing Brett Hawke; coach's experience).

## Kit

- Fins, paddles and a pull buoy replace part of the easy metres only. Threshold reps and tests are always without kit.
- With paddles or a buoy, ignore pace and hold effort. Small paddles; stop if a shoulder aches (Claude's judgement).

## Open Water

- **Without a wetsuit** body position matters more, especially late as fatigue sets in. Keep the head and chest cues going.
- **Sight and breathe:** lift the eyes on one stroke, breathe on the next. Lifting the head for 2–3 strokes can cost 5–10 s/100 m (Ford's estimate).
- **Chop:** stroke rate up, a bit scrappier.
- **Draft** on feet, not hips. Leave feet that are slower than your pace or that navigate badly. Drafting feels like less grip on the water but is faster for less effort.
- **Pool prep:** sight every 6–8 strokes at the wall end; swim close behind another swimmer. Add sighting to the Zone 2 part of foundation swims in Base 3, and open-water sessions in Build (Claude's judgement for the timing).
