# Strength & Durability

Strength work supports the endurance sessions; it never competes with them. Write each strength session as a `Sessions` row like any other, every exercise with sets, reps, load and reps in reserve (RIR), and the steps adding up to the planned minutes.

## Why Endurance Athletes Lift

- **Heavy strength improves cycling and running economy and performance** in endurance athletes when added to endurance training (Rønnestad and Mujika 2014 review; Luckin-Baldwin 2021 in triathletes; research).
- **Durability:** in a 2.5-hour fatigue protocol, runners with greater maximal leg strength kept their running economy better; higher aerobic fitness protected threshold and VO2max speeds (2026 study of 45 runners, described on That Triathlon Show; research, correlational only, paper not checked). Aerobic training matters more; strength is the second lever.
- **Age:** strength matters more for age-groupers than for young elites, because of age-related muscle loss (That Triathlon Show hosts; coach's experience).
- **Tendons and connective tissue adapt over months, not weeks** (Edgley, Galpin; coach's experience). Keep strength going all year; stopping and restarting loses the slow adaptations.
- **Plyometrics need strength alongside them.** Plyometrics alone have a trivial effect on running economy (effect size 0.19); combined with resistance training the effect is large (1.34), with bigger gains after more than 15 sessions over more than 7 weeks (Dudagoitia Barrio 2023 meta-analysis; research).

## Phases

| Strength phase | When                          | Sessions/week | Prescription                                                                      |
| -------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| AA             | Prep, Transition              | 2             | Light, many reps, learn the movements                                             |
| MT             | First weeks of Base, re-entry | 2             | 2–3 sets of 8–10 at 3 RIR, load rising each week                                  |
| MS             | Base 2–3                      | 2             | Main lower-body lifts 3 × 4–6 (hinge or hip thrust 3 × 6–8) at 2 RIR, ~2 min rest |
| SM             | Build, Peak                   | 1             | Main lifts 2 × 4–6 at the MS loads; short core circuit on a second day            |
| none           | Race week                     | 0             | A short core circuit once or twice is fine                                        |

Phase names follow Friel; the prescriptions are Claude's judgement within the research loads below.

- **Recovery weeks:** one set of each exercise at the same load.
- **Last session** about 10 days before an A race.
- **Once a week maintains** strength gains in-season (Rønnestad 2010; research).
- Elite Norwegian endurance coaches use 4–8 exercises, 2–4 sets, 5–10 reps, well short of failure, with little periodization (2026 survey of 12 coaches; research, descriptive). Controlled trials used heavier loads (about 80% 1RM, 2–3 RIR).

## Session Shape

In order (Galpin's programming order; coach's experience):

1. **Warm-up:** bird dog or a similar low-load core drill.
2. **Plyometrics** right after the warm-up, while fresh (see below).
3. **Main lifts:** single-leg press or split squat, hip thrust, a hinge (RDL) kept moderate, always with at least 3 RIR.
4. **Single-leg work weaker side first** (see "Left/right check").
5. **Upper body** with at least as many pulls as pushes: inverted rows, push-ups, dips. There is no evidence for an exact pull-to-push ratio; pulls get skipped when time is short, and Galpin has never seen an injury from too much pulling (coach's experience). Freestyle is front-of-shoulder dominant, which argues for the pulls (Claude's judgement).
6. **Calves:** bent-knee and straight-knee calf raises, for runners with calf, arch or Achilles history.
7. **Core built into the session** rather than a separate daily routine: dead bug, side plank, Pallof press, suitcase carry. Anti-extension progressions such as the ab wheel add range before reps; end the set when the hips drop or the low back takes over.

For an athlete with low-back or SI-joint pain: no loaded spinal extension (back extensions); keep the RDL well short of failure and swap it for a hip thrust when the back complains; stop any lift that sharpens the pain (Claude's judgement).

## Progression

- All sets at RPE 6 or less: add the smallest load step next week (about 2.5 kg or 5 lb).
- Any set at RPE 8.5 or more: repeat the load.
- Strength soreness that changes the next day's swim, bike or run means do less next time: drop one set and keep the load. Without such soreness, 2–3 RIR is fine (That Triathlon Show hosts; coach's experience).

## Placement in the Week

- The heavy lower-body day follows an easy endurance session and lands at least one day before the next quality or long session (Claude's judgement). Strength after the endurance session of the day, not before.
- An alternative is lifting on the same day as the hardest session, then finishing with a few strides and a longer cool-down to bring muscle tone down (Bakken; coach's experience). Either works; protect the quality sessions.
- Cold-water immersion straight after heavy strength may blunt strength gains (Bakken's own caveat); keep it away from strength days.

## Plyometrics

- Vertical, low-load drills first: pogos, snap-downs, countermovement jumps, then single-leg pogos. Box jumps, bounding and maximal skips come later, if at all.
- About 30–70 foot contacts per session, rising by about 10 a week; 60 s rest between sets; trainers on a hard, flat floor (Claude's judgement).
- End a set when contacts get loud or slow.
- **Stop rule:** pain at the athlete's tracked pain site that day or the next morning, or sore calves or Achilles, means repeat the previous week's dose.
- Unilateral plyometrics reduce left/right differences better than bilateral ones (8 small trials, about 150 people; Galpin's summary; research).

## Jump Rope

Jump rope in place of 5 minutes of the normal run warm-up, 2–4 times a week, 10–20 minutes a week in total, for 10 weeks improved 3-km time trial, jumping, reactive strength and arch stiffness in amateur runners (García-Pinillos 2020 RCT; research). The control group did no plyometrics, so an athlete already doing plyometrics should expect less.

- Part of the warm-up before easy or quality runs, not counted in training hours.
- Boxer skip or two-foot bounce, low, quick and quiet; 1-minute sets with 30 s rest; build from 2 × 1 min toward 5 × 1 min a session, then hold (Claude's judgement, inside the study's dose).
- The limit is calf and Achilles tolerance (about 100+ contacts a minute), not skill. Same stop rule as plyometrics. Heavy calves in the weekend long run mean moving the rope session earlier in the week.
- No double-unders until the basic dose is tolerated.

## Left/Right Check

Triathlon is a symmetrical sport, so a left/right difference in the legs is worth finding: a 10% difference in ground-contact time costs about 4–8% in running metabolic cost (Galpin's summary; research, mostly observational). Single-leg hop asymmetry is the best-supported injury screen (Galpin; research). Unstandardised tests give false flags, so test the same way every time.

Run it in each test week, before the strength session, same shoes, after the warm-up, legs alternating, best of 3:

1. Knee-to-wall distance in cm (ankle dorsiflexion)
2. Single-leg hop for distance, landing held 2 s (skip it if plyometrics are bothering the athlete)
3. Single-leg calf raise to fatigue, 1 set
4. Optional: side plank hold per side

Difference = (better − worse) ÷ better × 100. Galpin's function thresholds (coach's experience):

| Difference | Read   | Action                                                         |
| ---------- | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| under 10%  | green  | Leave it                                                       |
| 10–20%     | yellow | Weaker side gets one extra set on the matching exercise        |
| over 20%   | red    | Priority; extra weak-side set, and suggest a physio assessment |

Racing goals justify acting on yellows. Check a difference is real before correcting it: posture and bone length explain many apparent differences. Tight ankle: mobility between sets rather than a separate routine. Record each check in `Tests` (Sport: Strength, Protocol: left/right check).

## Soft-Tissue and Breathing Work

Rolling, massage guns and breathing work are the athlete's own practice. Use these principles when the athlete asks about soreness, mobility or recovery; schedule them only if the athlete asks (Jill Miller; coach's experience):

- **Comfortable discomfort:** the athlete can still breathe normally. A clenched jaw, fists or held breath means too much.
- Soft tools over hard ones; not on bone, spine, the front of the neck or a painful joint.
- Before training for range of motion and less next-day soreness; after or the next day is also fine. Rolling increases range of motion acutely and over weeks (Konrad 2022 meta-analyses; research). A massage gun on the calves for 5 minutes improved dorsiflexion without strength loss (Konrad 2020; research).
- Rolling alone is not the fix: pair it with strength work, sleep and food.
- Slow breathing with long exhales, about 5 minutes a day, lying down, is the parasympathetic practice.
